# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

A bake-off is judged on food that has to exist at a fixed time — a loaf baked
two days early or two days late is a different loaf, so there's no automatic
extension for a missed bench. If illness, an oven failure or something else
outside your control is going to stop you turning up, ask before the deadline,
not after it. Come with what you have: a genuine attempt with an honest
account of what went wrong is assessed on its merits.

## When a bake fails

A collapsed loaf isn't the same as a failed assessment. Fault diagnosis is
part of what's being judged — naming what went wrong, why, and what you'd
change is worth marks on its own, most explicitly in bake-off 2's criteria.
Bring in the bread you actually made, with the honest version of your notes,
rather than not turning up at all. Notes describing a bake that plainly didn't
happen cost you more than the bad bake would have.

## Academic integrity

The bread on the bench has to be the one you baked. Talking through a
formula, comparing hydration or troubleshooting a stall with another student
is normal kitchen practice and is fine here too, provided you declare it in
your notes — who you talked to and what changed as a result. Baking someone
else's dough, or bringing in bread someone else made, is not.

## Food safety and allergens

Bakes are cut and tasted on the bench, in front of the room and each other, so
treat every loaf as something someone else is about to eat. Declare any nuts,
dairy, egg or other common allergens before your bake is cut, not after. Keep
raw eggs and dairy refrigerated until they go into the dough, wash hands and
equipment between doughs, and don't bring in anything you wouldn't eat
yourself.

## Getting help

Say something before the deadline, not after it. Marisol and Idris would
rather hear about a dying starter or a broken oven on a Tuesday than see an
empty space on the bench on a Friday.
