Three baguettes, one bench, and no fat or sugar to hide behind. Everything you
taste on Monday comes from flour, water, salt, yeast and what you did with time.

## Before the session

Mix a poolish 12 to 16 hours ahead: 150 g bread flour, 150 g water at 20 °C,
and 0.15 g instant yeast — less than a pinch, so weigh it or scale a larger
batch. Cover it and leave it on the bench, not in the fridge.

It should arrive domed, webbed with bubbles, smelling of cream and cider. A
poolish that has sunk into a flat grey puddle fermented too far; bring it
anyway, and taste the difference in the crust against someone else's.

Bring a lame or a fresh blade, a linen couche or a clean tea towel, and a bench
scraper. Read the week 2 lecture on scoring and steam before you arrive — the
bench moves fast, and the geometry is easier to follow with dough in your hands
than off a slide.

## In the session

The bench runs to the clock, four hours, in this order:

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Autolyse — 350 g flour and 200 g water, mixed to a shaggy mass, then left alone |
| 0:30 | Add the poolish, 10 g salt, 3 g instant yeast; mix three minutes, no more |
| 0:35 | Bulk ferment, with folds at 30 and 60 minutes |
| 2:05 | Divide at 350 g, pre-shape into loose cylinders |
| 2:25 | Bench rest, covered, while the gluten relaxes |
| 2:45 | Final shape, then proof seam-down in a floured couche |
| 3:30 | Score and bake at 250 °C |

The half hour of autolyse buys back ten minutes of mixing: the flour hydrates
and gluten begins to link up without any mechanical help, and the dough comes
together smooth on a fraction of the work. Total hydration is 70%, salt 2%.

Shaping is the part worth slowing down for. You are not pressing the gas out —
you are stretching a skin. Fold the cylinder over on itself, seal each fold
under the heel of your hand, and roll out from the centre keeping that outer
surface taut. Pinch the seam properly closed. The loaf proofs and bakes
seam-down, so a seam that is merely laid shut unzips along the bottom in the
oven, and the blowout takes the crumb with it.

Score at 20 to 30 degrees, the blade almost lying along the dough, about 5 mm
deep, each cut overlapping the one before by a third. Steam for the first
10 minutes, then vent the oven and finish dry for another 10 to 12.

## Afterwards

Cut one loaf open while it is still ticking and photograph the crumb next to the
crust. Write down which of the three failures you got: the seam that unzipped, a
score made too deep or too vertical that tore flat instead of lifting, or a dull
matt crust that set before the loaf had finished rising because the steam never
arrived.

That note is the start of your evidence for
[bake-off 1](/assessments/bake-off-1-lean-doughs/), which is lean dough and
nothing else. Week 3 and week 4 are the bench time you have left before it.
