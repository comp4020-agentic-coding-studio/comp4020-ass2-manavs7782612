# Hydration and the open crumb

SLOP3422 · ciabatta

---

## Eight points past the baguette

| Hydration | What the dough does                     |
| --------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 55%       | stiff, tight crumb, hard work           |
| 65%       | holds a ball, takes a seam, week 1      |
| 70%       | rolls to 45 cm without snapping, week 2 |
| 78%       | flows to fill the tub, this week        |
| 85%       | a batter with ambitions                 |

Same four ingredients. Different substance.

---

## Slack is the point

Dilute gluten stretches further before it tears. Thin bubble walls are what an
open crumb is made of.

A stiff dough cannot produce this crumb at any fermentation length.

---

## You cannot knead soup

- no resistance to work against
- twenty minutes of slapping develops almost nothing
- a coil fold takes ten seconds and stretches the whole mass

Four folds, thirty minutes apart. The waiting does as much as the folding.

# The holes are gas you already made

Bulk fermentation produces them. Shaping only decides whether you keep them.

---

## Overnight arrives already fermented

- **poolish** — 100% hydration, pours into the mix, flavour and extensibility
- **biga** — 50% hydration, slower, more acid, the Italian habit

About 40% of your flour turns up pre-fermented either way.

---

## Wet hands, floured bench

Dough sticks to dry skin and to bare wood. Water on your hands, flour on the
bench, a plastic scraper for everything else.

Tip the tub out. Do not pull the dough.

---

## Cut and dimple

Straight down with the bench knife. No punch, no pre-shape, no seam.

The rectangle is the shape the dough was already in. Fingertips only, and only
on a piece domed enough to bake unevenly.

---

## Three ways it fails

- **puddle** — no folds, spreads to 15 mm, bakes flat
- **tight crumb** — degassed at shaping, holes thrown away
- **gummy centre** — pulled at 90 °C, the middle never finished setting
