# Sourdough

Week 6. The packet of yeast goes back in the drawer.

---

## What is in the jar

Bacteria outnumber yeasts about **100 to 1**.

- yeasts make carbon dioxide — the lift
- lactic acid bacteria make acid — the flavour
- pH 3.5 to 4.2 keeps everything else out

---

## Two acids, two breads

| Acid | Lactic | Acetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tastes like | yoghurt, milk | vinegar |
| Made when | 28 to 30 °C, slack, fed often | 18 to 22 °C, stiff, long intervals |

Sourness is a setting.

---

## Feed by ratio

**1:1:1** doubles in 4 to 6 hours at 24 °C and peaks an hour or two later. **1:5:5** takes two to three times as long.

Watch the curve: climb, dome, peak, collapse. Feed at the peak.

A once-a-day jar at 19 °C spends most of its day starving in acid.

# The clock does not know your kitchen is 18 °C

---

## Reading the end of bulk

- **30 to 50%** rise off the mark — not double
- domed, smooth, wobbles like set custard
- bubbles where the dough meets the tub wall

---

## Two ways to get it wrong

**Too early:** it finishes in the oven — a cavern under the crust, a blowout down the side.

**Too late:** proteases win — slack, flat, tears instead of opening.

---

## Cold, then very hot

Fridge at 4 °C overnight — flavour keeps developing, and cold dough scores clean.

Lidded pot at 250 °C — the loaf's own steam, trapped. Lid off to colour.
