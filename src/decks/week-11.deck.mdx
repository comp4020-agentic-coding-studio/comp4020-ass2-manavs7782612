# Banana bread

No yeast. No kneading. No proof.

---

## One reaction, and it starts when the batter is wet

NaHCO₃ + H⁺ → Na⁺ + H₂O + CO₂

1 g of soda → 0.52 g of carbon dioxide → about 290 mL in the bowl, 360 mL once
the crumb nears 100 °C.

Most of it is over in two minutes. Mix, fill, bake.

---

## Soda needs an acid. Powder brings one.

| Raising agent | Starting ratio | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baking powder | 1 tsp per 120 g flour | needs only liquid |
| Bicarbonate of soda | ¼ tsp per 120 g flour | needs acid in the batter |

Buttermilk, yoghurt, brown sugar, natural cocoa, ripe fruit. Not Dutched cocoa
— it has been alkalised.

The soda figure scales with the acid, not with the flour.

# Lumps are correct

Twelve folds. Stop when the last streak of dry flour goes.

---

## Beat it smooth and you build gluten

- close, rubbery crumb that resisted the gas
- long vertical tunnels where the gas channelled instead of spreading
- plain flour at 9%, with fat and sugar keeping the strands short

Rye had no gluten to give you. This batter has plenty, and you leave it alone.

---

## Too much soda, three symptoms

- **taste** — soapy, metallic, bitter, from leftover sodium carbonate
- **colour** — darker than the bake time warrants; alkali speeds Maillard
- **shape** — domes, cracks wide, then sinks; all the gas came before the
  structure set

---

## Black-spotted, or it does not work

Ripening is the banana's own amylase at work: 20% starch and 2% sugar green,
the reverse when the skin blackens. Pectin breaks down so it mashes. pH falls
to about 4.8.

An hour in a 150 °C oven blackens the skin and kills the enzymes. Soft, bland,
starchy, useless.

---

## 175 °C for 55 minutes

Deep tin, half water. At 200 °C the crust is dark at twenty minutes and the
centre is still batter.

Skewer with moist crumbs, 96 °C inside. The crack down the top is the crust
setting before the middle stops rising — leave it.

It is also the one component of a bake-off 3 showpiece you can mix and bake
inside the hour.
