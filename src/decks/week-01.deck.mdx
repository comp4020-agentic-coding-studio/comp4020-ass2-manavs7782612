# Flour, water, salt, yeast

SLOP3422 · the plain tin loaf

---

## Flour is 100 %

| Ingredient    | Bakers' % | For 1 kg flour |
| ------------- | --------- | -------------- |
| Flour         | 100 %     | 1000 g         |
| Water         | 65 %      | 650 g          |
| Salt          | 2 %       | 20 g           |
| Instant yeast | 0.7 %     | 7 g            |

The column sums to 167.7 %. That is not an error --- flour is the ruler, not the
whole.

---

## Gluten is two proteins and some water

- **glutenin** --- strength and elasticity
- **gliadin** --- extensibility, the ability to flow rather than snap

Dry flour has neither doing anything. Water hydrates them, mixing links them
into a network. Bakers' flour carries 11.5--13 % protein; plain flour around
9 %.

---

## Hydration is the dial

- **55 %** --- stiff, tight crumb, hard work by hand
- **65 %** --- handles cleanly, even crumb, this week
- **80 %** --- slack, open, sticky, and week 3's entire problem

# Salt is structural

2 % tightens the gluten and slows the yeast. Leave it out and the dough
ferments away from you before you have shaped it.

---

## The straight dough

mix → bulk 90 min at 24 °C → shape → proof 50 min → bake 230 °C

Every method after this week is a deviation from that line. Week 6 lengthens the
ferment, week 9 adds fat and egg, week 10 folds butter through it.

---

## Oven spring stops when the starch sets

- gas expands, water flashes to steam, yeast makes a last burst and dies
- from around 60 °C the starch gelatinises, the gluten sets, growth stops
- a hollow knock means the crumb is set and the walls are rigid

---

## Three ways it fails

- **under-mixed** --- the dough tears instead of stretching thin
- **over-proofed** --- the loaf collapses the moment it is scored
- **oven too cool** --- pale, leathery crust, no colour to taste
