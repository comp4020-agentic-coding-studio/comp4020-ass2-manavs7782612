# The skin set in water

Bagels. 55% water, 14% protein, cooked twice.

---

## The hydration goes backwards

| Week | Bread      | Hydration |
| ---- | ---------- | --------- |
| 1    | Plain loaf | 65%       |
| 3    | Ciabatta   | 78%       |
| 8    | Bagel      | 55%       |

Not enough water to lubricate the strands, so they link up slowly. Twelve
minutes on low, and the mixer walks.

---

## Malt does three jobs

- **colour** — there is no other sugar in the formula
- **flavour** — the malt note you cannot get from flour
- **food** — about two-thirds maltose, straight to the yeast

Non-diastatic only. Live enzyme does nothing at 4 °C and too much in the oven.

# Forty-five seconds a side

The first cooking is the one that decides what it is.

---

## What the water does

Surface starch gelatinises into a gel skin about a millimetre thick.

- a **lid** — oven spring is cut short, the crumb stays dense
- a **gloss** — tacky and sugar-rich, so seeds actually stick
- a **chew** — set in water, not in the oven

---

## pH sets the crust colour

| Boil water            | pH   | Crust                     |
| --------------------- | ---- | ------------------------- |
| Malt syrup            | ~6.5 | Warm tan, malty           |
| Bicarbonate, 1 tbsp/L | ~8.5 | Darker, faint pretzel     |
| Food-grade lye        | ~13  | Mahogany — not this bench |

Alkali makes the surface proteins far more reactive to Maillard browning.

---

## Three ways it fails

| What you see           | What happened                |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Wrinkled, leathery     | Over-boiled, or over-proofed |
| Dense puck, pale crumb | Boiled before it floated     |
| No hole left           | Never opened wide enough     |

Then 225 °C, dry. Steam is week 2's tool, not this one's.
